I've read the planning doc, all the phase notes, the G-CNN implementation, and the arena/screen setup. Bottom line: the engineering and experimental hygiene are genuinely excellent - but the stop conclusion is not supported by the evidence collected. The screen answer's "does this particular G-CNN implementation beat an ordinary CNN on 6,000 positions?" (no), and that answer was translated into "no justification for the fresh architecture," which neither follows logically nor is what the data shows. Here's the breakdown.

## What the agent did well

Real credit first: the phase gates were followed honestly, final test data and arena seeds were kept untouched, the corpus pipeline caught two genuine datagen bugs (trajectory game-ID leakage, terminal no_moves pseudo-actions), the calibration work produced a real finding (T~262 vs the nominal 400, with early-stage bias), and the decision to stop rather than lock a winner on ambiguous evidence is exactly the discipline the plan asked for. The infrastructure - corpus converter with differential validation, per-epoch selection, cluster-bootstrap arenas - is all reusable.

## Why the conclusion overreaches

1. The scale can't support an architecture verdict. The entire screen ran on 5,848 unique positions (10k draws with replacement), for 4-8 epochs of batch 256 - roughly 150-300 gradient steps, on local CPU, with training runs of ~100 seconds. The resulting models have 7-13% top-1 policy accuracy - barely above random-over-Legal-moves. Meanwhile the 100k-record pilot corpus (99,951 unique positions) sat 94% unused, and Pla's own numbers show datagen produces 100k records in 264 seconds - the data for a real screen costs minutes of CPU. Architecture rankings in a data-starved, undertrained regime routinely invert at scale, and the plan itself labeled these pilot ablations "screening experiments, not claims of final strength." The stop decision quietly promoted a screening result into a final-strength claim.

Why only 5,848 positions? The 20/35/45 stage-balance contract collided with a corpus that is only 1-4% early-stage, so the sampler shrank everything to the scarcest stratum. The right fix was generating early-heavy data (the --random-moves-min 0 mode built in Pla supports exactly this) or reweighting the loss - not starving the dataset.

2. The equivariant candidates were structurally handicapped - by an implementation choice, not by equivariance. In V4Prototype.py, the policy head applies a 1x1 conv reading only multiplicity channels per orientation branch - that's effective/8: 12 channels for candidate A, 24 for C, versus the 96 channels the ordinary control's policy head reads. The value head group-pools before its conv, so it also reads only 12-24 channels. This is equivariant (the tests pass - it's not the invariant-collapse trap) but it's an expressivity-starved cousin of it: a proper regularp_policy readout mixes all 8 group components with tied weights, an 8x wider input. On top of that, "matched effective channels" means the equivariant nets had 175k-510k parameters against the control's 1.35M - and on a 5.8k-position corpus, parameter count dominates the generalization dynamics. The screen's own data is consistent with eadout width being the binding constraint: candidate C (multiplicity 24) jumped to 11.1% top-1, above the controls 7.4% in the matched stage-blenc screen. I'll own a share of this: my design doc's sizing table framed comparisons in effective channels without specifying readout design or a param-matched variant, and that framing set up the handicap.

3. The logic of the stop inverts the bake-off's purpose. The ordinary control was added (at the reviewer's correct insistence) as a candidate - to test whether equivariance earns its complexity. "The control wins" is a legitimate bake-off outcome that selects the ordinary architecture, not a failure of the bake-off. The plan's fresh-start condition - "paired with the architecture upgrade" - was always capacity + encoding + oracle-in-loop recipe, with equivariance as one hypothesis inside it (my original words for the upgrade were "a bigger tower and/or richer input encoding"). Note what was never tested: no ordinary CNN larger than 8x96 was ever trained. The capacity axis - the core of the upgrade - has zero data points. An ordinary 10x128 or 6x192 with 13 planes, bootstrapped, with V3's existing (working) symmetry machinery retained, fully satisfies the fresh-start condition. The stop decision treats "equivariance failed its audition" as "V4 is dead," which is a non sequitur.

4. The question that actually decides V4 was never asked. No candidate - not even the winning control - was ever arenad against Run13. The entire bet rides on whether bootstrap pretraining lands within striking distance of Run13 before any self-play; a G1-preview arena of the ordinary-global control vs Run13 would have been 40 games and would tell you more about V4's viability than everything in Plb combined.

For completeness, things I checked that don't undermine the result: the C-vs-ordinary arena was fair (both nets at root-symmetries=1, random initial orientations - if anything this denied the ordinary net its V3-style ensemble boost, making its win more credible), the ordinary control did get D4 augmentation in training, and the 10-30 full-game result is statistically real between those two undertrained checkpoints. The screen also produced legitimately useful secondary results: global blend › winner-only and › stage blend at this scale, and 13 planes help policy.

## What I'd do next

1. Reframe the candidate set: V4 does not require equivariance. Add ordinary 10x128 and ordinary 6x192 (13-plane) candidates; keep V3's augmentation/root-averaging for them - deleting that machinery was a bonus of equivariance, never the justification for V4.
2. Scale the screen to where it can decide: generate a stage-corrected ~M corpus (a few hours of CPU, using the zero-random-move mode to fill the early stage), train on GPU, and plot learning curves at ~100k/300k/1M for the control, the bigger ordinary candidates, and - as a passenger, not a gate - one G-CNN with the readout bottleneck fixed and roughly param-matched. Decide capacity from curve separation; decide whether M is worth generating from curve slope.
3. Run the G1-preview arena vs Run13 for the best candidate. That's the go/no-go signal for the whole fresh-start bet.
4. Keep everything else the agent built - the pipeline, calibration, gates, and the untouched final splits are all still valid.
